PART III / THE PROOF

Why one half?

The proof is a contradiction. An off-line zero would make a particular prime-counting error too large. A construction involving many linked arithmetic maps shows that the error cannot be that large.

Start at the conclusion and follow its dependencies. Each step states what it needs from the next. The formulas on this page specialize to ordinary zeta where possible; the Lean proof handles all Dirichlet characters.

Two kinds of certainty. The formal proof is certified (21 November 2026). The classical guide by Rubinstein and collaborators, version 2, 22 November, does not yet derive the main arithmetic estimate from scratch. Our deeper explanations identify those gaps rather than silently assuming them. Sources checked 24 November 2026.

12 / EXCLUDE THE RIGHT HALF

Exclude the right half

If a nontrivial zeta zero lies to the left of the critical line, the functional equation places another zero to its right. It is enough to prove that the right half is empty.

ζ(s) ≠ 0   when   Re(s) > ½, s ≠ 1

Remove one half of the plane

Place a hypothetical zero anywhere in the strip. Its reflected partners are forced by the symmetries.

Hypothetical zeroForced partners

This is an exact implication of the symmetries, not a search for new zeros. Turn on the theorem: any off-line candidate contradicts it, even if the candidate was initially on the left.

Mathematical details The exact endpoint

The completed function ξ(s)=½s(s−1)π−s/2Γ(s/2)ζ(s) obeys ξ(s)=ξ(1−s). Its zeros are precisely the nontrivial zeros of ζ. Nonvanishing on the right therefore forces Re(s)=½. For Dirichlet functions the functional equation pairs a character with its conjugate. Imprimitive functions differ by finitely many factors 1−χ(p)p−s; these cannot vanish for Re(s)>0 because |χ(p)p−s|<1.

The five exported statements and their exact scope are recorded by Sutherland. They do not establish GRH for all automorphic L-functions.

13 / A ZERO CANNOT HIDE

A zero cannot hide

The prime sum G from Part II is designed so that a zero at β+iγ appears as a singularity of a transform. We do not need a uniform error bound everywhere; controlling G from above throughout occasional long windows suffices.

G(x) ≤ Cxθ   on   [Y,Y3/2]

If this holds for arbitrarily large Y, for a fixed ½<θ<1, the window criterion rules out zeros with real part greater than θ. If we can do it for every such θ, RH follows.

A pole inside the forbidden circle

The one-sided estimate controls the high derivatives of a transform. A hypothetical pole too close to the evaluation point makes those derivatives grow faster than the allowed bound.

Allowed scaleSingle pole

Exact single-pole Taylor coefficient, with θ=0.60 and σ=3 fixed. Constants and other poles are omitted here; the localization and isolation argument below is what justifies this comparison for the real transform.

Mathematical details Derive the transform and the pole

For Re(s)>1 integrate each ramp separately:

∫n∞(x/n−1)x−s−1dx = n−s/[s(s−1)]
F(s)=−ζ′(s)/[s(s−1)ζ(s)]−1/(s−1)²+(1+γ)/(s−1)

The last two terms remove the singularity at 1. At a zero ρ of order ν, write ζ(s)=(s−ρ)νh(s), h(ρ)≠0. Then ζ′/ζ=ν/(s−ρ)+h′/h. The transform has residue −ν/[ρ(ρ−1)]. In the general positive family of characters the numerator is a positive weighted sum of multiplicities, so the pole cannot disappear.

Why windows are enough — classical proof outline

Set u=log x, g(u)=G(eu), and Aj(s)=(−1)jF(j)(s)/j!. For a large real σ, the integral weight is uje−σu/j!. At U=log Y choose j near 5(σ−2)U/4. The weight after using the global estimate |g(u)|≪e2u concentrates exponentially in [U,3U/2].

|Aj(σ+it)|+Aj(σ) ≤ 2∫ max(g(u),0)uje−σu/j! du

Only the positive part of g is needed. The window bound controls the integral inside; the exponentially small tails control the rest. Along these j the right-hand side is O((σ−θ)−j) for σ sufficiently large. If a pole lies beyond θ, choose a center z=σ+it with a unique nearest pole at distance d<min(σ−θ, distance from σ to its nearest pole). This is possible by moving t near the height of a suitable nonreal pole. Pick r strictly between d and that minimum. The pole contributes a term of order d−j; after subtracting it, Cauchy's estimate bounds the remainder and |Aj(σ)| by O(r−j). As d<r<σ−θ the two bounds contradict one another for large j.

This is the ordinary-zeta classical argument, with constants and tail estimates in Part II §1 ↗; the Lean route is not literally this derivation. See the localization experiment.

14 / RECOVER ALL THE PRIMES

Recover all the primes

The arithmetic construction gives information about a restricted collection of primes. We need a way to recover the original count without paying an error larger than square-root scale.

Pick auxiliary primes q and d. Let χq and χd be their quadratic characters, with η=χqχd. For r coprime to qd, the weight (1+χq(r))(1−χd(r)) selects numbers which are squares modulo q and nonsquares modulo d. It is 4 there and 0 elsewhere.

Rq,d(x)=G(x)+Gχq(x)−Gχd(x)−Gη(x)

This is an identity of centered sums, including the exceptional residues with their actual weights. Estimate B controls the three unwanted terms. Estimate C controls the cells counted at much larger scales. With (M), these give Estimate A for R.

Extract a restricted prime sum

Every prime power contributes to four signed sums. Add them with signs +,+,−,− and watch all but the desired residue classes cancel.

Retained class (+,−)Other classes / exceptional

Exact raw weighted ramps using q=11, d=1319; odd primes other than 11, with weights (1+χ₁₁)(1−χ₁₃₁₉). The exceptional class χ₁₃₁₉=0 is included. Centering constants and the analytic estimates are separate.

The four actual error terms

This explores the four terms of the actual estimate, suppressing x to the O(ε) factors. Arbitrary slider choices need not satisfy the construction's constraints.

Mathematical details Estimate A and the limiting argument
Rq,d(x) ≪ε xCε(x/m+xm/k+m+k/n²)

At m=x1/2+O(ε), k=x9/2+O(ε), n=x2+O(ε), the four exponents are ½, −3, ½, ½ up to O(ε). Estimate B gives |Gχ(x)|≤x1/2+ε for χ in the three twists. Thus G(x)≪εx1/2+C′ε on the windows. Given a hypothetical β>½, choose θ between ½ and β, then ε small enough that ½+C′ε<θ. The constant is absorbed at sufficiently large x. No substitution ε=0 is justified or needed.

15 / THE INEQUALITY AT THE HEART OF IT

The inequality at the heart of it

The new arithmetic objects allow us to compare a weighted sum of prime powers with a predicted main term. It is the difference, rather than the size of either, that has to be small.

meanu ∑t,j(Pu,t,j−Mu,t,j) ≤ E
E/B ≤ xCε(x/m+xm/k+m+k/n²)

Here u translates a set of residue classes, t labels members of a family and j labels stages of a chain. The construction fixes weights w, positive derivative orders at and a normalizing quantity B. In the classical reconstruction the sums P contain nonnegative ramp contributions from prime powers. M is an independently derived main term.

A real residue table

Use q=11, d=1319, n=4, a=10,000. The eight cells classify odd prime powers by quadratic character modulo 11 and by the pair of residues ±r modulo 16. A distinguished cell has weight 10; the rest have weight 0.05.

χ₁₁(r)±1±3±5±7

Each cell shows its computed contribution to P. A weight of 10 cuts off at 1,000; weight 0.05 cuts off at 200,000. The finite example illustrates the sum, not the hypotheses of (M).

Here p is odd and p≠q, z+=max(z,0), cχd=L′(1,χd)/L(1,χd). In the full data set B=|T|mbδ/2, with δ=|T₂|/n. The sample takes one table, one stage, t=1; its fractional weights are for illustration only. 16 / BUILD MAPS, NOT INTEGERS

Build maps, not integers

Chebyshev compares prime divisibility with the size of a single integer. This proof uses maps of lattices of automorphic forms. A chain of theta lifts amplifies the divisibility contribution at each step.

U(X₀) → U(X₁) → ··· → U(Xₘ)

A theta kernel converts a function on one unitary group into one on another by integration or pairing. The arithmetic structure controls which denominators can appear; analytic structure controls its norm. The iterated lift has a final period which can be compared across different choices of intermediate twists.

Compose a family of maps

A chain is more than a sequence of arrows: the final map depends on the intermediate choices. Here each stage is an integer matrix with determinant 2. Tap a stage to switch its map.

Exact two-dimensional toy maps A = [[2,1],[0,1]], B = [[1,0],[1,2]]. Each composite has index 16 in the same target lattice ℤ², although the image and size change. Actual theta lifts act on much richer spaces; this experiment illustrates composition and common target, not their construction.

Mathematical details The maps and their arithmetic data

In the classical reconstruction, each word w specifies successive choices of characters and maps. Its composite βw maps a glued source lattice Λwgl into a common target R∨. The architecture uses the same target for a family of maps, which is essential for the rank comparison below. The local theta packages, identities of integrals, local matching and compatible unitary groups are separate inputs, not consequences of the Gaussian toy sum.

17 / WHY ODD PRIME POWERS APPEAR

Why odd prime powers appear

At primes inert in a suitable complex multiplication field, repeated differentiation creates systematic powers of p. The shape of the resulting valuation is remarkably close to the ramp used to count primes.

a/(p−1)−a/(p²−1)=a(p⁻¹+p⁻³+p⁻⁵+···)

See the odd powers emerge

The factorial produces contributions at every power of p. Subtract the loss from the formal logarithm and compare what remains with the sum at odd powers alone.

Odd powersEven powers / loss

Exact Legendre sum and coefficient-loss bound ⌊(a−1)/(p²−1)⌋ in the formal-logarithm model below. The odd-power series is the leading term, not an exact equality of integer valuations. The genuine theta-kernel estimate requires additional arithmetic inputs.

Mathematical details A local calculation with a formal logarithm

As a model take ℓ(X)=X+∑j≥1Xp2j/pj and its formal inverse U. Set L=p²−1. The coefficient of za in U has p-adic valuation at least −(a−1)/L. Induct on degree in U=z−∑Up2j/pj: a product of r=p2j coefficients costs at most (a−r)/L, and division by pj adds j≤(r−1)/L.

For integral f, the same product estimate applies to f(U(z)). The a-th derivative multiplies the coefficient by a!, whose valuation is ∑⌊a/ph⌋. Subtract the coefficient loss and obtain the displayed odd-power series up to O(1+logpa). The weighted lattice estimate introduces (a/ph−w)+=w(x/ph−1)+ when a=xw.

The model calculation is elementary; it does not establish integrality of the actual kernels or the local losses when passing to lattices. Part II §2 ↗, §III.5 ↗, §III.6 ↗.

18 / MATCH THE MAIN TERM

Match the main term

The divisibility estimate is useless unless the same maps have a sharp analytic size bound. Getting only the leading term is not enough: an unmatched error proportional to x would swamp the desired square-root error.

∫₀∞ tae−tdt=a!

The Gaussian norm gives factorials. Stirling's expansion includes a log a − a, not merely a log a. Period formulas, inner-product formulas and lattice covolumes supply further constants that must match the arithmetic main term.

Measure the index with area

An integer map stretches the unit square into a parallelogram. The area counts the index of its image in the original integer lattice. Change the two diagonal entries and the shear.

Image latticeFundamental parallelogram

Exact matrix [[a,b],[0,d]]. Index = ad; the determinant is bounded by the product of column lengths and by the square of the operator norm. Shear increases the size bounds without increasing the index. The proof generalizes this comparison to slopes and covolumes.

Mathematical details From one determinant to slopes

For an injective map A between equal-rank Euclidean lattices Λ and R, write I=[R:AΛ]. Then I·covol(R)=|det A|covol(Λ). If each singular value is at most L, log I≤d log L+log covol(Λ)−log covol(R). For unequal ranks the relevant target is the saturated image; the proof uses slope inequalities for lattices and controls the mean slope through a family. This identity is a finite-dimensional model of the comparison, not its replacement.

The actual size estimate uses theta inner products, period identities, local indices and dimension/covolume estimates. §III.4 ↗, §III.7 ↗ give the conditional derivation and enumerate the needed inputs.

19 / ONE MAP IS NOT ENOUGH

One map is not enough

A map into a large lattice may only illuminate a small subspace. Even a spectacular divisibility estimate in that subspace would not control the full target. Many images must cover almost the entire space without all pointing in the same direction.

When do many maps give new information?

Imagine six unit vectors, each with a distinct direction plus a shared component. As the shared component grows, their images overlap more. The Gram matrix measures this directly.

Distinct directionsShared direction

Exact Gram matrix: diagonal 1, all other entries c. Its eigenvalues are 1+5c and five copies of 1−c. The effective-rank lower bound is 6/(1+5c²), not the actual rank unless at the endpoints. Relative trace estimates play the role of controlling overlaps in the real proof.

Mathematical details A second-moment mechanism

For unit vectors v₁,…,vN spanning dimension r, let A=∑vᵢvᵢ*. Its eigenvalues are nonnegative, tr A=N and tr A²=∑|⟨vᵢ,vⱼ⟩|². Cauchy–Schwarz yields r≥N²/tr A². If every vector points in the same direction, the bound is 1; if they are orthogonal, it is N. Control of overlaps is therefore an effective way to prove a rank bound.

The actual proof uses relative trace-formula estimates for periods, together with the subspace and size conditions; this elementary Gram argument only illustrates the mechanism. §III.8 ↗; announcement item 16.

20 / KEEP THE MAPS INTEGRAL

Keep the maps integral

The intermediate theta maps have rational denominators. An integer divisible by many primes is valuable; a rational number can erase that divisibility in its denominator. The proof varies intermediate twists and combines integral structures prime by prime to limit the loss.

Combine two integral structures

One choice of intermediate map may have a bad denominator at 2, and another at 3. Consider two admissible sublattices d₁ℤ and d₂ℤ of the same target ℤ. Their sum keeps the smaller valuation at each prime.

First choiceSecond choiceSum of lattices

Exact one-dimensional model: d₁=2ᵃ3ᵇ, d₂=2ᵇ3ᵃ; their sum is gcd(d₁,d₂)ℤ. Bézout's identity produces the generator. The higher-dimensional theta problem has extra obstructions, controlled by cohomology; one cannot simply replace it with a gcd.

Mathematical details The arithmetic obstruction

Changing an intermediate character may preserve the final period while changing its local denominators. One seeks a common integral structure built from these choices, so that no single choice of denominator controls the entire argument. The residual obstruction is measured using lengths of locally trivial Galois cohomology groups of twisted modules. Their weighted sum controls the denominator losses in the formal comparison.

The definition VVle requires the relevant local lengths to be finite and their logarithmically weighted sums bounded. The statement vvRecord converts this to a summable bound. The cohomology comparison itself is a deep input, not an elementary gcd identity. §III.9 ↗ and §III.11 ↗ distinguish the conditional calculation from the quoted theorem.

21 / MAKE IT WORK AT EVERY SCALE

Make it work at every scale

The argument needs a complete construction for every x inside arbitrarily late windows. Having good data at just one scale, or for one character, would not establish RH.

Auxiliary quadratic characters are selected using estimates in analytic number theory. Behrend-type sets of residue classes give the combinatorial supply. A cohomological window provides the uniform arithmetic controls; a host-existence theorem narrows that window to the one required by the analytic argument.

Choose many classes without a progression

The arithmetic supply uses sets that avoid short additive relations. Here encode triples of digits (0–4) as a number in base 10. Select a sphere in the digit cube to see why progressions disappear.

Exact finite Behrend example: 2b=a+c causes no carries in these digits, so an arithmetic progression of distinct numbers would give three collinear points on the same Euclidean sphere. Strict convexity forbids that. The proof needs larger sets avoiding more general bounded-coefficient relations and compatible arithmetic hosts.

Mathematical details The quantifiers and the bridge

Fix a primitive character ψ. The property VWinAt ψ 401 supplies a cohomological window of the form [Y,Y²] under the stated scale conditions. The theorem supplyChi consumes that property to obtain HostExW(3/2) for the required data. These are quantified over sufficiently large scales; the choices may depend on x and on ψ, but the final family and window must satisfy the analytic criterion. A definition such as VWinAt is not by itself a theorem of existence.

22 / CLOSE THE LOOP

Now read it forward.

Supply the data, build the theta chains, control their local divisibility, norms, ranks and denominators. The resulting estimate bounds a weighted prime error. Character comparison and parameter choices produce the required one-sided windows. Mellin localization excludes every zero to the right; reflection excludes every zero to the left.

The most striking aspect is the change of object: a question about zeros of a complex function is answered by finding enough arithmetic maps with the right divisibility and size.
